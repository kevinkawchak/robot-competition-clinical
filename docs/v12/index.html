Clinical Robot Competition v1.2.0 — Real-Life GCP Trial Suite II

Four autonomous stations compete to execute a complete GCP-aligned intramuscular injection visit on realistic human trial participants. Each station is staffed by two real Unitree H2 humanoids — the actual robot geometry, all 75 revolute joints and dual SHARPA dexterous hands, converted from the official H2_Plus/H2_with_sharpa.usdz robot description in kevinkawchak/fork_unitree_model. A human Clinical Research Associate (CRA) observes from the sponsor monitoring desk. The suite is open-air (no ceiling) under a physical daylight sky, and the camera zooms infinitely from any point — scroll straight through a needle bore or out past the building.

What is new in v1.2.0

  • Real robot files — Unitree H2 Plus visual meshes + full joint tree (axes and limits from the USD physics joints), not a hand-modeled approximation
  • Naturally dissimilar stations — per-seed motion styles (stride, cadence, lean, reach, head energy, staggered starts) and profile-correlated scores; no two stations move or score alike
  • Realistic patients — rebuilt human participants with articulated five-finger hands, expressive faces, ECG chest leads, personas (calm / talkative / anxious / elderly) and idle body language
  • Infinite zoom — fly-through dolly with no minimum or maximum distance, zoom-to-cursor, adaptive near/far planes
  • Sharper signage — all posters, labels and boards re-rendered at 4× resolution with auto-fit headings (nothing clips)
  • Real-life suite — daylight sky, wall bumper rails, wall clock, crash cart, scrub sink, supply shelving, gas outlets, EXIT signage, plants

12-Phase GCP Workflow (per station)

  1. Identity verification — nurse scans wristband (two identifiers)
  2. eConsent confirmation — nurse presents tablet, participant nods
  3. Baseline vitals — nurse captures ECG/SpO2/BP on live monitor
  4. Hand hygiene & gloves — doctor performs hand rub
  5. Drug preparation — doctor draws 0.50 mL at the supply cart, barcode check
  6. Aseptic site prep — doctor swabs the right deltoid
  7. Landmark & alignment — needle aligned to target (IK-corrected)
  8. Needle insertion — 90° IM, tip meets the deltoid surface exactly
  9. Dose delivery — plunger depresses, 0.50 mL over slow push
  10. Withdraw & safety — needle out, safety engaged
  11. Sharps disposal — syringe dropped in the yellow sharps container
  12. Observation & eSource — nurse documents; AE surveillance

Composite GCP Scoring

  • Time (30%) — total visit duration across all 12 phases
  • Accuracy (25%) — recorded needle placement deviation (mm)
  • Protocol adherence (20%) — deviations from the visit schedule
  • Sterility (10%) — aseptic technique index
  • Dose precision (10%) — delivered vs 0.500 mL target
  • Participant comfort (5%) — motion-jerk proxy

One station experiences a seeded Grade 1 adverse event (transient vasovagal episode) during observation: its ECG dips, the nurse responds, and the extended observation costs time — demonstrating autonomous AE surveillance and response.

PPO Policies

All stations run the same PPO architecture (MLP 64×64, tanh) with unique seeds (42, 137, 256, 512). Reward R = −0.3×time + 0.5/(1+dist) + 0.2/(1+jerk). Same policy, different state → distinct speed/accuracy/protocol trade-offs, and in v1.1.0 the seeds also drive visibly different motion styles.

Why This Matters for Clinical Trials

Parallel autonomous stations executing the full GCP visit — identity, consent, vitals, dosing, AE surveillance, and eSource documentation — show how physical-AI sites could compress oncology trial timelines versus sequential human-staffed visits, while keeping a human CRA in the monitoring loop.
